Before you start trading, it’s essential to understand the core principles behind every Moneta Funded challenge. These rules ensure fairness, transparency, and consistency across all accounts — keeping the evaluation process equal for every trader.

View Rules

Key Challenge Rules
To help you perform your best, Moneta Funded provides clear and measurable trading parameters. Each rule defines the boundaries of professional risk management and ensures traders progress based on skill, not chance.

Daily Drawdown
In the course of one working day, the maximum daily drawdown on the account (which consists of the account’s current result of opened positions and the result of positions closed on the given day) is calculated based on the balance or equity, whichever is greater when the day changes at 10pm UTC, minus the daily loss amount for the account size. The daily loss amount is calculated based on the initial account size, not the current balance or equity. The Daily drawdown is 3% for Instant, 1-Step, and Phoenix; 4% for Instant Pro; and 4% or 5% for 2-Step, depending on the add-on.

This includes commissions and swap fees. If the account at any point breaches the daily drawdown, the account is breached.

Daily Drawdown
Max Drawdown
The maximum total drawdown is 5% trailing of the initial balance for Instant; 8% trailing for Instant Pro; 6% static for 1-Step and Phoenix; and 8% or 10% static for 2-Step, depending on the add-on. Therefore, for 2-Step for example, the equity of the account must not fall below 90% of the initial account balance both for open and closed positions, including commissions and swaps.

Please note that customers are unable to utilize their total available drawdown during a single day of trading. Therefore, customers cannot go over the 5% daily drawdown within 24 hours of trading, and the 10% max drawdown is only applied to multiple trading days.

Any violation of the rules above will result in the termination of your trading account and you will not be eligible to move forward in the program with that account.

Max Drawdown
Profit Target
Profit Target is the amount of profit traders must achieve in a specific phase of the challenge to graduate to the next phase of the challenge. Importantly, the profit target is calculated based on closed positions, meaning it doesn’t include floating/pending positions.

Profit Target varies across challenges and phases, with profit target set to 10% for Phoenix, 1-Step and 2-Step Phase 2 and 5% for 2-Step Phase 1.

Profit Target
Minimum Profitable Days
We set the minimum profitable days across all challenges to 3 days. This means traders must trade for at least 3 days in each phase (excluding Instant Funding) in order to pass a given challenge. Each trading day must achieve a profit of 0.5% to qualify as a profitable trading day.

Minimum Profitable Days
Inactivity Rule
Inactivity for all accounts is set to 30 calendar days. Therefore, a trader must place a single trade within a 30 calendar day period to avoid the inactivity rule going into effect, which would result in their account being automatically breached. There are no exceptions to this rule.

Inactivity Rule

How does my Daily Permitted Loss work
How does my Daily Permitted Loss work
At Moneta Funded, we use a specific logic for our Daily Permitted Loss to ensure responsible risk management.

June 2, 2026
⚠️ Important Points

The Daily Loss Limit is calculated based on your Balance or Equity, whichever is higher, at the rollover snapshot, minus the Daily Loss amount for your account size.

The Daily Loss amount is calculated based on your initial account size.

A violation occurs if your Equity drops below the calculated Breach Level.

Example: On a $100,000 account with a 5% Daily Loss Limit, the Daily Loss amount is $5,000. If your Balance or Equity at the rollover snapshot is $101,000, your Daily Loss Breach Level will be $96,000 ($101,000 − $5,000).

The Daily Permitted Loss resets every day at 10 PM UTC (rollover).

We do not recommend trading during the rollover period, as spreads may widen and volatility may increase.

Rollover Window: The Daily Permitted Loss always resets at 10 PM UTC (rollover). During this period, it is not recommended to trade.

How the Snapshot Works
At 10 PM UTC, our system takes a snapshot of your account and compares your Balance and Equity. While the Daily Loss Limit is calculated based on your Balance or Equity, whichever is higher, a violation occurs if your Equity drops below the calculated Breach Level.

 

Note: It is not recommended to trade during the rollover period due to market volatility and spread widening.
​ 

 

Scenario 1: Trade Open in Profit During Rollover
Example based on a $100,000 account with a 4% Daily Loss Limit = $4,000

 

At 10 PM UTC, the system takes a snapshot of your account while you have an open trade with floating profit.

Balance: $100,000
​(The trade is still open, so the Balance remains unchanged.)

Equity: $101,000
​(Balance + $1,000 floating profit)

The Calculation
The system uses the higher value, which is your Equity of $101,000, to calculate the Daily Loss Limit for the new trading day.

 

$101,000 - $4,000 = $97,000 Breach Level

 

The Rule
Your Equity cannot be lower than $97,000 for the new trading day.

 

What This Means
Since your Equity at rollover was $101,000, you have a $4,000 buffer from that snapshot level.

 

Because your Balance was still $100,000, this also means that the floating profit included in the snapshot affects the Breach Level. In practical terms, if your open trade moves enough against you and your Equity drops below $97,000, the account will be considered in breach.

Scenario 2: Trade Held in Loss During Rollover
Example based on a $100,000 account with a 4% Daily Loss Limit = $4,000. 
At 10 PM UTC, the system takes a snapshot of your account while you are holding an open trade in loss.
Balance: $100,000

Equity: $99,000
​(Because you have a -$1,000 unrealized loss)

The Calculation
The system uses the higher value, which is your Balance of $100,000, to calculate the Daily Loss Limit for the new trading day.

 

$100,000 - $4,000 = $96,000 Breach Level

 

The Rule
Your Equity cannot be lower than $96,000 for the new trading day.

 

What This Means
Since your Equity at rollover was already $99,000, you now have a $3,000 buffer before reaching the Breach Level.

 

If further floating loss or closed loss causes your Equity to drop below $96,000, the account will be considered in breach.

 

Key Reminder
Although the Daily Loss Limit is calculated using the higher of your Balance or Equity at rollover, the actual violation is based on Equity falling below the Breach Level.

 

Because of this, traders should always monitor Equity, not just Balance, especially when holding open trades during rollover.

Overview of Available Trading Platforms at Moneta Funded

Updated over 3 weeks ago
MetaTrader 5 (MT5):
MetaTrader 5 (MT5) is a multi-asset trading platform widely used by prop trading firms for its advanced analytics, speed, and compatibility with automated trading systems. It supports trading in Forex, and CFDs with robust order management, depth of market (DOM) data, and powerful charting tools. For prop firm traders, MT5 provides a familiar and professional environment for executing trades, testing strategies, and tracking performance under real or simulated conditions. Its built-in MQL5 programming language allows firms to create custom indicators, scripts, and expert advisors (EAs) for automated strategies.

 

 

Match-Trader:
 

Match-Trader is a modern, all-in-one trading platform developed by Match-Trade Technologies, designed to serve both brokers and prop trading firms. Unlike MetaTrader 5 (MT5), Match Trader is built as a web-based Progressive Web App (PWA) with synchronized access across devices and includes built-in TradingView charts for advanced technical analysis. 

 

The platform emphasizes transparency and ease of use, offering tools for both manual traders and prop firms running account challenges. 

 

Please note that MT5 is only available to non-US Traders due to regulations, while Match-trader is available to both US traders and non-US Traders. 

 

Trade execution may differ slightly between platforms, but all orders are subject to Bid/Ask pricing, available simulated liquidity and slippage. Learn more about how trades are executed.

What Are Moneta Funded’s IP Rules?
What Are Moneta Funded’s IP Rules?
Rules for normal IP changes, personal devices, VPN access, and private VPS use.

Updated over 2 weeks ago
At Moneta Funded, we understand that traders may use personal devices or trade from different locations. Our IP policy is designed to remain flexible for normal account access while protecting the integrity and ownership of every trading account.


Using Different IPs and Devices
You are free to:

Trade from different IP addresses as part of normal personal use

Use of personal devices

Switch between your home connection, mobile data, or another normal private connection

IP changes are completely normal and do not violate our rules.

However, we recommend avoiding free VPNs and public or shared VPS services. Another Moneta Funded trader may be assigned the same IP address, which could raise concerns that multiple trading accounts are being managed by the same person and may result in an account review.

 

VPN and VPS Eligibility by Country
VPN and VPS use is permitted only in the following countries:

China

Russia

Turkmenistan

Syria

These exceptions are provided because local internet restrictions may make it difficult to access the Moneta Funded website or trading platform through a normal connection.

For traders in all other countries, the use of a VPN or VPS to access or trade a Moneta Funded account is not permitted. Traders should use their normal private internet connection.


Private VPS Requirements
Where VPS use is permitted, the VPS must be for your exclusive personal use.

Do not use a public or shared VPS.

Do not share the VPS login, remote-desktop access, trading credentials, or account access with another person.

Do not use a VPS that is also being used by another Moneta Funded trader.

If another Moneta Funded trader is assigned the same VPS or IP address, the activity may appear as though multiple accounts are being managed by the same person. This may result in an account review.


Examples
Allowed example: 

Personal VPS in an approved country

A trader in China is unable to reliably access MT5 through their normal connection. The trader rents a private VPS for their own exclusive use and personally operates only their own Moneta Funded account.

 

Why this is allowed:

China is one of the approved countries, and the VPS is private and used only by the registered trader.

Allowed example: 

Normal IP and device changes

A trader uses home Wi-Fi on a laptop and later uses mobile data on their own phone. The IP address changes, but both devices and connections belong to the same trader.

 

Why this is allowed:

Natural IP changes and personal devices do not violate the rules.

Not allowed example: 

VPN or VPS from a non-approved country

A trader in Germany uses a VPN or VPS to hide or replace their normal connection when accessing the dashboard or trading platform.

 

Why this is not allowed:

VPN and VPS use is limited to China, Russia, Turkmenistan, Syria.

Not allowed example:

Shared VPS

A trader in China uses a public VPS that is also used by several other traders, including other Moneta Funded users.

 

Why this is not allowed:

Even in an approved country, a VPS must be private and used exclusively by the registered trader.

 

What We Monitor
Our primary concern is multiple accounts being used from the same IP address or trading environment in a way that may indicate account management, shared control, or access by multiple people.

 

Our team may review activity involving:

Multiple Moneta Funded users connecting through the same VPN or VPS IP

Public or shared VPS environments

VPN or VPS access from a country where it is not permitted

Unusual access patterns that suggest an account is being controlled by someone other than the registered trader

If clarification is needed, our team may contact the trader before determining the appropriate next step.

 

 

CID (Computer ID) Monitoring
In addition to IP addresses, we also monitor CID (Computer ID), a unique identifier tied to a specific device.

 

If multiple users or accounts are associated with the same CID, this may raise concerns related to:

Prohibited trading activity

Account management

KYC or identity-verification issues

How does my Daily Permitted Loss work
How does my Daily Permitted Loss work
At Moneta Funded, we use a specific logic for our Daily Permitted Loss to ensure responsible risk management.

June 2, 2026
⚠️ Important Points

The Daily Loss Limit is calculated based on your Balance or Equity, whichever is higher, at the rollover snapshot, minus the Daily Loss amount for your account size.

The Daily Loss amount is calculated based on your initial account size.

A violation occurs if your Equity drops below the calculated Breach Level.

Example: On a $100,000 account with a 5% Daily Loss Limit, the Daily Loss amount is $5,000. If your Balance or Equity at the rollover snapshot is $101,000, your Daily Loss Breach Level will be $96,000 ($101,000 − $5,000).

The Daily Permitted Loss resets every day at 10 PM UTC (rollover).

We do not recommend trading during the rollover period, as spreads may widen and volatility may increase.

Rollover Window: The Daily Permitted Loss always resets at 10 PM UTC (rollover). During this period, it is not recommended to trade.

How the Snapshot Works
At 10 PM UTC, our system takes a snapshot of your account and compares your Balance and Equity. While the Daily Loss Limit is calculated based on your Balance or Equity, whichever is higher, a violation occurs if your Equity drops below the calculated Breach Level.

 

Note: It is not recommended to trade during the rollover period due to market volatility and spread widening.
​ 

 

Scenario 1: Trade Open in Profit During Rollover
Example based on a $100,000 account with a 4% Daily Loss Limit = $4,000

 

At 10 PM UTC, the system takes a snapshot of your account while you have an open trade with floating profit.

Balance: $100,000
​(The trade is still open, so the Balance remains unchanged.)

Equity: $101,000
​(Balance + $1,000 floating profit)

The Calculation
The system uses the higher value, which is your Equity of $101,000, to calculate the Daily Loss Limit for the new trading day.

 

$101,000 - $4,000 = $97,000 Breach Level

 

The Rule
Your Equity cannot be lower than $97,000 for the new trading day.

 

What This Means
Since your Equity at rollover was $101,000, you have a $4,000 buffer from that snapshot level.

 

Because your Balance was still $100,000, this also means that the floating profit included in the snapshot affects the Breach Level. In practical terms, if your open trade moves enough against you and your Equity drops below $97,000, the account will be considered in breach.

Scenario 2: Trade Held in Loss During Rollover
Example based on a $100,000 account with a 4% Daily Loss Limit = $4,000. 
At 10 PM UTC, the system takes a snapshot of your account while you are holding an open trade in loss.
Balance: $100,000

Equity: $99,000
​(Because you have a -$1,000 unrealized loss)

The Calculation
The system uses the higher value, which is your Balance of $100,000, to calculate the Daily Loss Limit for the new trading day.

 

$100,000 - $4,000 = $96,000 Breach Level

 

The Rule
Your Equity cannot be lower than $96,000 for the new trading day.

 

What This Means
Since your Equity at rollover was already $99,000, you now have a $3,000 buffer before reaching the Breach Level.

 

If further floating loss or closed loss causes your Equity to drop below $96,000, the account will be considered in breach.

 

Key Reminder
Although the Daily Loss Limit is calculated using the higher of your Balance or Equity at rollover, the actual violation is based on Equity falling below the Breach Level.

 

Because of this, traders should always monitor Equity, not just Balance, especially when holding open trades during rollover.

Which Trading Methods Aren't Allowed?
Comprehensive overview of restricted trading strategies (Standard Challenges only)

July 8, 2026
We support a wide range of trading strategies, including trading assistant tools and EAs. However, Martingale, Reverse Trading, Copy Trading between accounts of different users, High-Frequency Trading, Hyperactivity and Excessive Order Spamming, Hedging, Grid Trading, Latency Arbitrage, Demo environment exploitation, Gamification of the Evaluation, and account sharing are strictly prohibited. These methods pose excessive risks and violate Moneta Funded's commitment to responsible trading. Use of any prohibited strategies will result in immediate account termination. Cheating, fraud, poor risk management, or exploitation of the simulated environment are also forbidden under our Terms & Conditions.



Detailed Overview of Prohibited Trading Strategies
Risk Management & Trading Conduct
Moneta Funded prioritizes disciplined, responsible, and sustainable trading practices. All trading activity is expected to reflect a genuine trading strategy that applies risk management principles a reasonable and prudent market participant would use when trading their own capital.

 

In situations where trading activity indicates elevated or inconsistent risk management, Moneta Funded may apply temporary risk parameters to one or more accounts (for example, a temporary risk-per-trade limit). These measures are preventative and are intended to help maintain stable and sustainable trading conditions. For more information, please refer to our article: Temporary Risk Limits on Funded Accounts.

 

Trading behavior that deviates from responsible risk management standards may be deemed non-compliant. This includes:

Allocating a substantial or majority portion of the allowable daily loss to a single trade idea, whether through realized losses or stop-loss exposure.

Allocating most or all available margin to a single trade idea or set of highly correlated positions.

Practices that undermine capital preservation, increase the probability of outsized losses, or rely on exposure patterns inconsistent with long-term trading sustainability are not permitted. Moneta Funded reserves the right to classify such activity as prohibited or non-compliant and to take appropriate action, including account restriction, termination, or disqualification from future participation.

 

Martingale Trading
Martingale-style trading refers to strategies where position size or overall exposure is increased after losses with the intention of recovering previous losses through a single winning trade. This approach is considered high-risk because it can quickly lead to disproportionately large losses if the market continues to move against the position.

Occasional and modest price averaging or position adjustments as part of normal trade management are acceptable, provided that overall risk on the trade remains controlled and does not escalate significantly. However, repeatedly increasing trade size after losses, concentrating a large portion of the account into recovery trades, or escalating exposure during drawdowns may be considered Martingale-type behavior and may trigger risk controls or account limitations.

Strategies that rely on progressively increasing position size to recover losses are incompatible with environments that operate under defined loss limits. Trading approaches must remain aligned with controlled and sustainable risk management at all times.

Reverse Trading
Reverse trading, whether conducted between different proprietary trading firms or internally between multiple Moneta Funded accounts, is strictly prohibited. Upon detection of reverse trading activity, Moneta Funded reserves the right to shut down the trader's account and ban the trader from future use of Moneta Funded's services.

High Frequency Trading
High-Frequency Trading (HFT) refers to trading patterns that rely primarily on extremely short holding times, rapid order placement, or execution speed rather than genuine market direction or strategy. This may include automated or manual strategies designed mainly to exploit latency, price feed differences, or execution mechanics through very high volumes of rapid trades.

Short-term trading and scalping are permitted. Trades held for seconds or minutes as part of a legitimate discretionary or systematic strategy are considered normal trading activity. However, trading behavior that consistently involves extremely short-duration trades executed in very high frequency, or strategies built primarily around execution speed rather than market analysis, may be classified as HFT and may trigger risk controls or account limitations.

These restrictions exist to prevent unfair execution advantages, excessive system load, or trading patterns that are not consistent with genuine market participation. All trading activity should reflect a genuine trading strategy with reasonable execution and risk management.

Hyperactivity and Excessive Order Spamming
Trading activity that involves an abnormally high volume of orders, excessive scaling, or repetitive order placement without a genuine discretionary trading rationale is prohibited. Hyperactivity designed to exploit execution mechanics, platform behavior, or evaluation rules rather than express a legitimate market view may result in account review and appropriate action.

 

Grid Trading or Hedging
Grid Trading or Hedging is a trading approach where opposing buy and sell orders for the same instrument are strategically placed to achieve equivalent or similar risk. This strategy carries the risks of market manipulation, excessive leveraging, market instability, and the potential for a risk-free simulated profit.

Latency Arbitrage Trading
Latency Arbitrage Trading involves employing a data stream intentionally designed to have a delay or lag in providing market data, such as stock prices or trading volumes. This grants an unjust advantage to the trader compared to others who must rely on real-time market data.

Employing Latency Arbitrage is deemed unethical and runs contrary to the ways in which legitimate financial markets function.

General Exploitation of a Demo Environment
While Latency Arbitrage Trading is one of the most frequented methodologies utilized to exploit demo environments, it's not the only way traders can exploit a demo environment in order to make unethical gains that wouldn't otherwise be possible within legitimate financial markets. That is, traders can also take advantage of glitches that arise within a demo environment rather than reporting the glitch taking place.

For example, if the demo feed suddenly stops at 2:00 price while at the same time allowing a trader to place trades locked in at the 2:00 price and consistently clicks back to where the feed should be 5 min later, if the trader looks at the other charts that don't contain a bug and places a trade on that exact 5 min window to exploit the glitch (as opposed to reporting the bug), this type of behavior would also be deemed a general exploitation of a demo environment.

Moreover, general exploitation of a demo environment violates our Terms & Conditions and will result in a trader's account being canceled as well as banning that trader from future use of Moneta Funded's services.

Gamification of the Evaluation
Participants must engage in genuine market speculation based on their own discretionary analysis. Any attempt to optimize, manipulate, or "game" the parameters of the evaluation instead of trading the financial markets is prohibited. This includes statistical or probability-based approaches that seek to exploit the firm's rules without a genuine market thesis, trigger, or understanding. Trading strategies whose primary objective is to maximize the probability of passing the evaluation by exploiting the program's mechanics, rather than demonstrating authentic trading skill, are not permitted.

 

Account Sharing

What Is Copy Trading & Is It Allowed?
Learn how copy trading works and when it is permitted on Moneta Funded accounts.

July 28, 2026
What Is Copy Trading?
Copy trading is a feature that allows trades placed on one account, known as the master account, to be automatically replicated on one or more other accounts, known as copied or “slave” accounts.


When a trade is opened, modified, or closed on the master account, the same action is mirrored on the connected copied accounts.


Copy trading may also be carried out manually by placing the same trade on another account, possibly with a slight delay.

MASTER ACCOUNT
The original trade is placed here
For example, a EURUSD BUY trade is opened, modified, or closed.

 

 

 

 

 

⟶
COPIED ACCOUNT
The same action is replicated here
The connected account mirrors the master account automatically or manually.

Is Copy Trading Allowed?
Yes, but only when the master account and every copied account meet all of Moneta Funded’s ownership and account-location requirements.

IMPORTANT CLARIFICATION
A Moneta Funded account may act as a copied or “slave” account only when the master account is also a Moneta Funded account and both accounts belong to the same individual.

This means you cannot use an account at another prop firm, a personal brokerage account, a signal provider, or another external service as the master account and copy those trades into a Moneta Funded account.


Requirements for Compliant Copy Trading
To comply with Moneta Funded’s rules, the master account and all copied accounts must meet every requirement below:

1
 All accounts must be with Moneta Funded
The master account and every copied account must be Moneta Funded accounts.

2
All accounts must be under the same name
The registration details must identify the same account owner.

3
All accounts must belong to the same user
Copying cannot be used to coordinate trades or share risk between different people.

 
 

Allowed Examples
ALLOWED
One trader copies between two Moneta Funded accounts
Alex owns Moneta Funded Account A and Moneta Funded Account B. Both accounts are registered under Alex’s name. Account A is the master, and Account B copies its trades.


​Why this is allowed: Both accounts are with Moneta Funded and belong to the same individual.

ALLOWED
The same user manually repeats a trade
Maria opens a GBPUSD trade on one of her Moneta Funded accounts, then manually places the same trade on another Moneta Funded account she owns.


​Why this is allowed: Manual copying is still copy trading, but both accounts are Moneta Funded accounts owned by the same user.

 

Not Allowed Examples
NOT ALLOWED
Another prop firm is used as the master account
Jordan places trades on an account with another prop firm and automatically copies them into a Moneta Funded account.


​Why this is not allowed: The Moneta Funded account is acting as the copied account, but the master account is not with Moneta Funded.

NOT ALLOWED
A personal brokerage account is used as the master
Sam trades on a personal broker account and connects a copier so the same trades are placed on a Moneta Funded account.


​Why this is not allowed: The Moneta Funded account is acting as the copied account, but the master account is not with Moneta Funded.

NOT ALLOWED
Trades are copied from a friend or family member
Taylor allows a friend to use Taylor’s Moneta Funded account as a copied account for trades placed on the friend’s account.


​Why this is not allowed: The accounts belong to different individuals, even if permission was given.

NOT ALLOWED
A signal provider or external service controls the copied trades
A trader connects a Moneta Funded account to a third-party signal service that places or mirrors trades from an external master account.


​Why this is not allowed: The trades originate from a third party or external service rather than the trader’s own Moneta Funded master account.

DIFFERENT OWNERS ARE NOT PERMITTED
For standard challenges, copy trading between accounts owned by different individuals, groups, or third parties is not permitted, even when the account owner has given permission. This includes friends, family members, signal providers, and external services.

 

 

Quick Compliance Check
Before connecting or manually copying trades, confirm all three statements:

The master account is a Moneta Funded account.

Every copied account is also a Moneta Funded account.

All master and copied accounts belong to and are registered under the same individual.

WHEN IN DOUBT
Do not connect the accounts until you have confirmed that the setup meets all three requirements.

 

What Happens If the Copy Trading Rules Are Violated?
Any form of copy trading that violates these rules may result in account violations, suspension, or disqualification.

How A Profitable Day is Calculated
How A Profitable Day is Calculated
This is an article on how the daily profitable day is calculated.

July 28, 2026
A profitable day is a day on which the closed positions made a positive profit of at least 0.5% of the initial balance (NET PROFIT = [Total profit - Total Loss] of the day = 0.5% profit).

 

The positive profit is calculated as follows: Minimum (Midnight Balance, Midnight Equity) - Previous Day Balance

 

Eg: Account size of $100K for you to gain a profitable day you will need to close the day with a min of $500 net in profit. 

 

$100,000 * 0.005 = $500  

 

Here is a detailed explanation of how to calculate it: 

 

Required Daily Profit Amount = Initial Account Balance × 0.005 

Initial Account Balance: The balance you started with when the account was first issued (e.g., at the beginning of a challenge phase).

Detailed Steps to Determine a Profitable Day.

 

To determine if a specific trading day counts as a "0.5 profitable day," follow these steps: 

 

1. Identify the Base Balance: Confirm the relevant balance according to the specific rules you are following (usually the initial account size).

 

2. Calculate the Target Profit Amount: Multiply the base balance by 0.005 (which is 0.5%) to find the exact dollar amount needed for that day.

 

Example 1: For a $100,000 account, the target is $500 for the day as the 0.5%

Example 2: For a $10,000 account, the target is $50 for the day as the 0.5%

3. Track Net P/L for the Trading Day: Throughout the designated trading day (usually from midnight to midnight in the platform's server time), track the net profit or loss from all closed positions. This can be achieved through multiple small trades or one larger move.

 

4. Compare Net P/L to the Target: At the end of the day, if your net profit amount is equal to or greater than the target amount calculated in Step 2, the day qualifies as a "0.5 profitable day". 

 

Important Considerations and Rules 

Consistency, Not Sequence: These profitable days do not typically need to be consecutive; you just need to accumulate the required number of qualifying days (commonly three) to meet a program objective.

Overall Balance Constraint: Some firms have an additional rule that even if you achieve 0.5% profit in a single day, it will not count if your account's overall balance is still below the original starting balance (e.g., due to previous losses), but that’s not the case in Moneta Funded: regardless of whether you’re above or below the original starting balance, the 0.5% profit in a single day will count for any day achieved.

Risk Management: This rule encourages consistent and sustainable trading performance rather than relying on a single lucky high-risk trade. It emphasizes managing risk to achieve small, steady gains. 
​
​Example (10% profit target):
Assume your account size is $100,000, so a 10% profit target equals $10,000.
The program requires 3 qualifying profitable days, but they do not need to be consecutive.

Day 1: +$3,500 (qualifying day ✅)

Day 2: −$1,200 (loss ❌ — doesn’t matter)

Day 3: +$2,000 (qualifying day ✅)

Day 4: +$4,500 (qualifying day ✅)
Therefore, the objective met through consistency over time, not consecutive winning days.

How Does the Consistency Rule Work at Moneta Funded?
How the consistency rule works (applicable to Instant Funding only — not Instant Funding Pro).

Updated over 3 weeks ago
What Is the Consistency Rule?
The consistency rule is a risk-management guideline designed to ensure profits are earned steadily over time rather than from a single oversized trading day. It limits how much of your total profit can come from any one trading day, encouraging disciplined, repeatable performance.

 

The consistency rule applies to Instant Funding accounts only. It does not apply to Instant Funding Pro, 1-Step, 2-Step, or Phoenix accounts.

 

 

Choosing Your Consistency Percentage
When you purchase an Instant Funding account, you select your consistency percentage: 15% or 20%. This is the maximum share of your total profit that may be generated on a single trading day. The 15% option is offered at a lower price than the 20% option.

 

 

How Is the Rule Calculated?
Your highest single-day profit must not exceed your selected percentage of your total profit:

 

Maximum allowed single-day profit = your selected percentage (15% or 20%) × total profit

 

The consistency percentage on your dashboard shows where you currently stand against this limit.

 

Detailed Example
A trader earns a total profit of $10,000:

On the 20% option: 20% of $10,000 = $2,000 — the largest single day must not exceed $2,000.

On the 15% option: 15% of $10,000 = $1,500 — the largest single day must not exceed $1,500.

How This Works in Practice (using the 20% option, $2,000 limit)
If the trader's highest single-day profit is $1,200, the rule is met, since $1,200 is below the $2,000 limit.

If the trader's highest single-day profit is $2,500, the rule is violated — even though the total profit target has been reached. The trader must keep trading to bring their single-day share back within the consistency percentage shown on the dashboard before they can pass and advance further.

(On the 15% option, the same trader's limit would be $1,500 instead of $2,000.)

 

 

Consistency Is Not a Minimum Profitable Days Rule
Important: Instant Funding has no Minimum Profitable Days requirement.
The 15% and 20% consistency options can mathematically require profits to be spread across several days, but this is not the same as a Minimum Profitable Days rule. There is no requirement for an Instant Funding trading day to reach 0.5% of the initial account size.

 

Consistency Rule After a Payout
For a funded account, the consistency rule is reset after each payout, so the calculation starts fresh for the next payout cycle.

Trailing Drawdown (Instant Funding & Instant Funding Pro)
Trailing Drawdown (Instant Funding & Instant Funding Pro)
How the trailing drawdown works for Instant Funding and Instant Funding Pro accounts.

July 28, 2026
What Is Trailing Drawdown?
Trailing drawdown is a maximum loss limit that moves up with your account as it grows, locking in a portion of your gains. Unlike a static drawdown — which is fixed to your initial account balance — a trailing drawdown follows the highest equity your account reaches, up to a limit.


At Moneta Funded, trailing drawdown applies to Instant Funding and Instant Funding Pro accounts. (The 1-Step, 2-Step, and Phoenix programs use a static drawdown instead.)



How It Works
Your maximum loss level starts at your initial balance minus the trailing drawdown amount.

As your account reaches new equity highs, the maximum loss level trails upward by the same amount.

The maximum loss level never moves down — once it trails up, it stays there.

Trailing stops once the maximum loss level reaches your original starting balance. From that point, the level is fixed at the starting balance and does not trail any higher.

If your balance or equity ever falls to the maximum loss level, the trailing drawdown has been reached and the account is breached.



Instant Funding
Instant Funding accounts use a 5% trailing drawdown.

 

Example — $100,000 Instant Funding account (5% trailing):

Account started: maximum loss allowed = $100,000 − $5,000 = $95,000

Account grows (+$5,000): balance is now $105,000. Calculated level = $105,000 − $5,000 = $100,000. Because this equals your starting balance, the trailing drawdown has reached its cap → $100,000

Account grows more (+$10,000): balance is now $115,000. Calculated level = $110,000, but the drawdown cannot exceed the starting balance → $100,000

Account drops: if equity falls to $100,000, the trailing drawdown is reached and the account is breached.


Note: The 5% trailing drawdown applies to Instant Funding accounts purchased on or after 2 July 2026. Instant Funding accounts purchased before 2 July 2026 use a 6% trailing drawdown — subtract $6,000 instead of $5,000 in the steps above (starting maximum loss level of $94,000).



Instant Funding Pro
Instant Funding Pro accounts use an 8% trailing drawdown. The trailing mechanic is identical to Instant Funding — only the percentage differs.

Example — $100,000 Instant Funding Pro account (8% trailing):

Account started: maximum loss allowed = $100,000 − $8,000 = $92,000

Account grows (+$5,000): balance is now $105,000. Calculated level = $105,000 − $8,000 = $97,000

Account grows more (+$10,000): balance is now $115,000. Calculated level = $115,000 − $8,000 = $107,000, but the drawdown cannot exceed the starting balance → $100,000

Account drops: if equity falls to $100,000, the trailing drawdown is reached and the account is breached.


Key points
Trailing drawdown is based on balance or equity, whichever is higher.

The maximum loss level only ever moves up, never down.

Trailing stops at your original starting balance.

The trailing drawdown level is not affected by payouts.

Temporary Risk Limits on Funded Accounts
Temporary Risk Limits on Funded Accounts
July 28, 2026
At Moneta Funded, all trading is expected to reflect disciplined, professional, and sustainable risk management practices. Our objective is to provide a realistic trading environment that rewards consistency while protecting both traders and the firm from excessive exposure.

 

Temporary risk parameters are part of our broader Risk Management & Trading Conduct standards and are designed to support long-term trading success and account stability.
​

Why Temporary Risk Limits May Be Applied
In certain situations, our Risk team may apply temporary risk parameters to one or more funded accounts if trading activity indicates that risk is being concentrated too heavily into individual trade ideas or overall account exposure.

 

These measures are preventative in nature and are intended to help maintain stable trading conditions while allowing the trader to continue operating within the program under structured and controlled risk.

 

Temporary limits — such as caps on the amount of risk permitted per trade idea — are widely used across professional trading environments to encourage disciplined trading and reduce the likelihood of outsized losses resulting from excessive exposure.
​

 

Summary
Moneta Funded may apply a 1.5% risk-per-trade-idea limit on funded accounts when trading behavior shows excessive concentration of risk.

 

This may happen if a trader reaches:

more than 3% floating loss on the same symbol in a 2-Step Funded Account

more than 2% floating loss on the same symbol in a 1-Step Funded, Instant Funding, or Phoenix account

another breach or violation of restricted trading methods or risk-management rules

*If the trader has multiple funded accounts, only the funded account that exceeds the 2% or 3% floating-loss trigger will be moved to the 1.5% risk-per-trade-idea limit.

 

When the temporary risk limit is applied, the trader must keep each trade idea within the 1.5% risk limit.

 

If the trader exceeds the 1.5% limit after the restriction is applied, the affected funded account will be breached.

 

This temporary risk limit may be removed after 14 days and 20 trades, as long as the trader remains within the limit during that period.

 

For more details about the floating-loss trigger, please see Max Floating Loss Allowed on Funded Accounts.

 


When Temporary Risk Limits May Be Introduced

 

Temporary risk parameters may be introduced where trading activity raises risk management concerns or deviates from sustainable trading practices. This may include, but is not limited to:

Martingale-style risk escalation or loss-recovery position sizing

Grid-style or averaging strategies that materially increase total exposure

Trading behavior inconsistent with our Risk Management & Trading Conduct standards

Concentration of a significant portion of account risk into a single trade idea

Multiple positions creating concentrated exposure on highly correlated instruments

These examples are illustrative and not exhaustive. Temporary limits may be applied whenever necessary to maintain stable and sustainable trading conditions.
​


Benefits of Structured Risk Limits

When applied, a temporary risk-per-trade framework is intended to support traders in maintaining consistent and sustainable performance. These measures help:

Encourage structured and repeatable risk management practices

Reduce the impact of individual trade losses on overall account performance

Promote long-term account stability

Help traders maintain their funded accounts by preventing oversized losses

Reinforce disciplined position sizing and exposure control

Many professional trading firms implement similar safeguards to support consistent risk allocation and account longevity.
​


What a Temporary Risk Limit May Include

If applied, temporary risk limits may restrict the amount of capital that can be placed at risk on a single trade idea or group of related positions.

Such limits may also apply across multiple funded accounts held by the same trader, particularly where overall exposure is concentrated.
​

 

Duration of Temporary Risk Limits
Temporary risk limits can be removed once the trader has completed both of the following:

14 days under the temporary risk limit

20 trades on a separate funded account while remaining within the limit

The trader must continue to follow all funded-account rules during this period. If the account exceeds the 1.5% risk-per-trade-idea limit while the restriction is active, the affected funded account will be breached.
​


How Risk Is Evaluated?
Risk per trade idea is generally assessed based on the maximum potential or realized loss of a position or group of related positions if the trade reaches its protective stop or logical invalidation level.

Multiple positions that collectively create concentrated exposure on the same or closely correlated instruments may be considered a single trade idea for risk evaluation purposes.

Max Floating Loss Allowed on Funded Accounts
At Moneta Funded, traders are expected to manage risk responsibly on all funded accounts.

July 6, 2026
One important part of this is avoiding situations where a single trade idea, or multiple trades on the same symbol, uses a substantial portion of the account’s allowable daily loss.

 

This applies to floating loss, not only closed loss.

 


What is the max floating loss trigger?
If a trader reaches the following floating loss on the same symbol in a funded account, the account will be automatically restricted:

 

2-Step Funded Accounts (5% Daily Loss option) and Instant Funding Pro: 3% of the account size in floating loss on the same symbol

2-Step Funded Accounts (4% Daily Loss option): 2% of the account size in floating loss on the same symbol

1-Step Funded Accounts, Instant Funding, and Phoenix: 2% of the account size in floating loss on the same symbol

 


What happens if this limit is triggered?
If the floating loss trigger is reached:

all open trades on the affected funded account will be closed automatically

the trader will receive an automatic email notification

the trader will not be able to place more trades until the next daily reset

the account will be moved to a 1.5%-of-account-size risk-per-trade-idea limit

The daily reset time is 22:00 UTC.

 

The first 2% or 3% floating loss trigger is treated as a warning and risk-control action. It is not an immediate account breach.

 

However, once the account is moved to the 1.5% risk-per-trade-idea limit, this limit becomes strict.

 

 

What is the 1.5% risk-per-trade-idea limit?
The 1.5% risk-per-trade-idea limit means that a trader must not allow floating loss on one symbol or trade idea to exceed 1.5% of the account size on the affected funded account.

 

If the trader exceeds the 1.5% limit after being moved to this restriction, the affected funded account will be breached.

 

This is no longer a warning.

 

 

Does this affect all of my funded accounts?
No. If a trader has multiple funded accounts, only the funded account that exceeds the 2% or 3% floating-loss trigger will be moved to the 1.5% risk-per-trade-idea limit.

 

If the trader later exceeds the 1.5% limit, only the funded account that exceeds the 1.5% limit will be breached.

 

 

Can the 1.5% risk limit be removed?
Yes.

 

The 1.5% temporary risk limit can be removed after:

14 days

20 trades on a separate funded account

The trader must remain within the temporary risk limit during this period.

News Trading at Moneta Funded
News Trading at Moneta Funded
Is news trading allowed, which events are restricted, and which instruments are affected.

Updated over 3 weeks ago
Overview
Trading around major economic news releases carries heightened risk. During high-impact events, markets can experience sudden volatility, price gaps, slippage, and widened spreads. These conditions make entries and exits difficult to control and can produce results that do not reflect a trader's genuine skill or strategy. For these reasons, Moneta Funded restricts news trading on most account types.



Is News Trading Allowed?
Restricted on all programs, except Instant Funding Pro.

Standard programs (1-Step, 2-Step, Instant Funding, Phoenix, Sprint) News trading is restricted on both the challenge (evaluation) and funded phases.

Sprint Challenge: news trading is restricted, and in addition gap trading, market open, and market close trading are also not allowed. The market open/close restriction covers the New York, London, and Asia session opens and closes, and the general CFD rollover at 10:00 PM UTC.

Instant Funding Pro: news trading is fully allowed; the restrictions below do not apply.

 

What Counts as a News Trade? (The 10-Minute Window)
A trade is treated as a "news trade" based on its timing relative to a scheduled high-impact (red folder) economic event affecting that instrument. We use the ForexFactory economic calendar (forexfactory.com) as the reference for scheduled high-impact events and their release times.


Restricted window: You may not open or close a trade — manually or via Take Profit / Stop Loss — during the 5 minutes before and the 5 minutes after a scheduled high-impact news event.


Allowed: A trade that was opened at least 2 hours before the scheduled event may close during the restricted window, including closure triggered by Take Profit or Stop Loss.


Platform Times (Important for the News Window)
ForexFactory shows event times in the timezone you select on the site. To apply the 5-minute window correctly, convert the event time to your trading platform's server time:

MetaTrader 5 (MT5): server time is UTC+3 in summer (daylight saving) and UTC+2 in winter.

MatchTrader: server time is UTC.

Always confirm the event time against your platform's clock before opening or closing a position on an affected instrument.


Risks Associated with Medium or High-Impact News Trades
Summary: Trading during high-impact news exposes the account to conditions that are difficult to manage and are not representative of normal market behavior:

Volatility spikes — prices can move sharply and unpredictably in seconds.

Slippage — orders may fill at a materially worse price than requested.

Price gaps — the market can jump straight through stop-loss and take-profit levels.

Widened spreads — the gap between Bid and Ask can widen significantly around the release.

Execution uncertainty — pending and stop orders may trigger at unexpected levels.

Spreads, liquidity and execution prices may change quickly around major news events. Learn more about market execution and slippage in Why Can My Trade Be Executed at a Different Price?



Which Instruments Are Affected?
When a currency has a scheduled high-impact (red folder) event on the ForexFactory calendar, the restriction applies to every instrument tied to that currency — its currency pairs, the indices and commodities linked to that economy, and its cryptocurrency pairs. The affected instruments differ by platform, so the mapping is shown separately for MT5 and MatchTrader below.


The affected currencies follow the ForexFactory high-impact (red folder) set: USD, EUR, GBP, JPY, AUD, CAD, CHF, NZD, and CNY. Only red-folder events are restricted — medium-impact events are not.


MetaTrader 5 (MT5)
includes Forex, Gold, Silver, Cryptocurrency, CFDs (Indices), Nikkei, Oil, and Commodities





MatchTrader
includes Forex, Cryptocurrency, Commodities, and Indices



Full symbol reference: What Can I Trade on Moneta Funded. Metals, oil, and soft commodities are USD-denominated and therefore listed under USD; gold/silver crosses (e.g. XAUEUR, XAUJPY, XAUAUD, XAGAUD) appear under their quote currency.



How to Check Upcoming News — Economic Calendar
Moneta Funded uses the ForexFactory economic calendar (forexfactory.com) as the reference for scheduled high-impact (red folder) events. Filter the calendar to high-impact events, note the scheduled release time, and avoid opening or closing positions on any affected instrument within the 5-minute window before and after that time. Always confirm the event time in your own timezone before trading.

 


 

Consequences of Violating the News Trading Rule
Net profits generated from trades that violate this rule may be subject to deduction.

Repeated attempts to trade high-impact news across multiple days may result in account restriction or violation.


Weekend Holding
Holding positions overnight and over the weekend is allowed on Moneta Funded accounts (except where a program restricts it — for example, gap trading on the Sprint Challenge). There is no requirement to close positions before the weekend.


Important — a weekend gap can trigger a daily loss breach. When the market reopens after the weekend, prices can gap significantly from Friday's close. If you are holding an open position, this gap can create a large floating loss the moment the market opens. Because the Daily Loss Limit is measured on your equity (including floating/unrealized losses), a weekend gap against your position can push your equity below the daily breach level and breach the account. Size any position you hold over the weekend with this gap risk in mind.

Should I Use a Stop Loss?
Updated over 3 weeks ago
A stop loss is a predefined price level at which a trade is automatically closed to limit potential losses. It is one of the most commonly used risk management tools in trading, helping traders control downside risk and avoid large, unexpected drawdowns.

 

Stop Loss orders are executed at the best available price once triggered and may be affected by slippage. Learn more in Why Can My Trade Be Executed at a Different Price?

That said, we strongly recommend using stop losses as part of a disciplined trading approach. Stop losses help protect your account, enforce consistent risk management, and reduce the emotional pressure that can lead to impulsive decisions.

 

While you are free to manage risk in your own way, traders who use stop losses tend to maintain more stable performance and are less likely to breach drawdown limits.

How Drawdown Works at Moneta Funded
How Drawdown Works at Moneta Funded
How drawdown works for the different accounts types at Moneta Funded.

July 28, 2026
Drawdown is the maximum loss your account is allowed to reach. At Moneta Funded, different programs use different drawdown models, so it is important to understand which one applies to your account.

 

Important Clarification
1-Step Challenge → Static Drawdown

2-Step Challenge → Static Drawdown

Phoenix Challenge → Static Drawdown

Instant Funding → Trailing Drawdown​

Instant Funding Pro → Trailing Drawdown​
​

 

Static Drawdown 
(1-Step, 2-Step, and Phoenix Challenges)

 

What Is Static Drawdown?
Static drawdown is a fixed loss limit calculated from your starting account balance. It does not change, even if your account grows.

 

Example
Starting Balance: $100,000

Maximum Drawdown: 10% ($10,000)

Lowest Allowed Equity: $90,000

If your account grows to $110,000, your maximum drawdown still remains $90,000. 

 

A violation occurs if your Equity drops below $90,000. This means the drawdown level stays fixed regardless of profit made after the account is opened.

 

*For the 2-Step Challenge, static drawdown is 10% on the Standard option or 8% on the lower-cost option (4% daily / 8% max). 1-Step and Phoenix use their own program-specific static levels.
​

 

Trailing Drawdown
(Instant Funding and Instant Pro)

 

What Is Trailing Drawdown?
A trailing drawdown is a dynamic loss limit that follows your account’s highest level as you trade. Unlike static drawdown, which stays fixed from the starting balance, trailing drawdown moves upward as your account grows. 

 

The purpose is to protect gains while still enforcing risk limits. The drawdown continues to move up until it reaches the starting balance, after which it no longer trails higher. This behavior is described in Moneta Funded’s separate Instant Funding trailing drawdown article.

 

Example (Instant Funding)
Before you start trading:

Starting Balance: $100,000

Trailing Drawdown: 5% ($5,000)

Starting Lowest Allowed Equity: $95,000

Once you start trading:

Current Equity: $103,000

Trailing Drawdown: 5% ($5,000)

Lowest Allowed Equity: $98,000

The trailing drawdown continues to move up as your account reaches new highs, until it reaches the starting balance ($100,000), after which it no longer trails higher.

 

As your account reaches new highs, the trailing drawdown moves upward with it. A violation occurs if your Equity drops below the active trailing drawdown level.

 

*NOTE: The 5% trailing drawdown applies to Instant Funding accounts purchased on or after 2 July 2026. Accounts purchased before 2 July 2026 use a 6% trailing drawdown.

 

Instant Funding Pro
Instant Funding Pro accounts also use trailing drawdown, with their own limits:

Maximum loss (trailing): 8% of balance or equity, whichever is higher

Daily loss: 4% of balance or equity, whichever is higher

As with Instant Funding, the trailing drawdown moves up as the account reaches new equity highs, but it never trails above the original starting balance.

Example — $100,000 Instant Funding Pro account (8% trailing):

Account started: maximum loss allowed = $100,000 − $8,000 = $92,000

After a $5,000 profit (balance $105,000): maximum loss allowed = $105,000 − $8,000 = $97,000

After a further $10,000 profit (balance $115,000): calculated $107,000, but the trailing drawdown cannot exceed the original starting balance, so it caps at $100,000

If the account then falls to $100,000, the trailing drawdown has been reached and the account is breached.

 

 

 

Key Reminder
Before trading, make sure you understand which drawdown model applies to your account. Static and trailing drawdowns work differently, and understanding the difference is important for managing your risk correctly.

Why Can My Trade Be Executed at a Different Price?
Understanding Bid and Ask prices, simulated liquidity, market depth, slippage and execution latency.

Updated over 2 weeks ago
Core execution principle
Moneta Funded accounts operate in a simulated trading environment designed to reflect real-market execution conditions as closely as reasonably practicable.

The price displayed on the platform is the current best Bid or Ask price. However, that price may only be available for a limited volume. If the full order cannot be executed at that price, the remaining volume may be processed at subsequent price levels, resulting in a different average execution price.

 

 

Why Can Execution Differ From the Displayed Price?
Moneta Funded's Programs are designed to simulate real-market conditions. As part of this simulation, execution may reflect changing liquidity, Bid/Ask spreads, execution latency, partial fills and price slippage.

 

Free Trial and Competition Accounts
Important exception
Free Trial and Competition accounts use a simplified demo execution model and do not apply simulated slippage or execution delay.

 

Execution on these accounts may therefore differ from Challenge and Funded accounts, which are designed to more closely reflect real-market execution conditions through simulated liquidity, slippage and execution latency.

 

Bid and Ask Prices
Every instrument has two prices:

Buy positions open at the Ask and close at the Bid.

Sell positions open at the Bid and close at the Ask.

Many charts display only the Bid price. Therefore, the execution price may appear different from the price visible on the chart.

Stop Loss example
A SELL position is closed using the Ask price. The Ask may reach a Stop Loss even when a chart that displays only Bid appears not to have touched that level.

 

What Is Slippage?
Slippage is the difference between the best Bid or Ask price when an order is submitted or triggered and the final execution price.

 

Liquidity may be distributed across several price levels. If the full order volume is not available at the best price, the order may be processed using the next available prices.

 

Example
Available Ask Price

Available Volume

4030.20

2 lots

4030.30

2 lots

4030.40

3 lots

 
A 5-lot market BUY may be processed as:

Execution Price

Filled Volume

4030.20

2 lots

4030.30

2 lots

4030.40

1 lot

 
Average execution price: approximately 4030.28

This example is for illustration only. Actual execution depends on the instrument, order size and conditions at the time the order is processed.

 


Can Slippage Occur During Normal Market Conditions?
Yes. Slippage is not limited to news releases or periods of high volatility. It may occur because of:

available liquidity;

order volume;

spread changes;

price updates during execution;

execution latency.

Slippage may be positive or negative. A difference between the best Bid or Ask price and the final execution price does not automatically indicate a platform error.

 

How Does Slippage Affect Stop Loss Orders?
A Stop Loss is a trigger level, not a guaranteed execution price.

Once triggered, the closing order is processed at the best available simulated execution price. During fast price movement, a gap, reduced liquidity or a changing spread, the final closing price may differ from the Stop Loss level.

This is why the Stop Loss level shown on the order and the final closed price are not always identical.

 


What Is Spread Widening?
The spread is the difference between the Bid and Ask price. The spread can widen when liquidity changes or market activity increases.

 

A wider spread can affect both the price at which an order triggers and the account's floating Equity. This is especially important for positions close to a Stop Loss, Daily Loss level or Maximum Loss level.
​

 

What Happens During News, Rollover and Weekend Gaps?
During major news events, session transitions, rollover periods and weekend reopenings, prices and available liquidity may change rapidly. This can result in widened spreads, price gaps, slippage and different execution prices.

RISK MANAGEMENT REMINDER

If a market gaps beyond a Stop Loss level, the Stop Loss can trigger but the trade may close at the next available simulated execution price rather than the requested Stop Loss price.
For program-specific news and gap restrictions, traders should also review the relevant News Trading and account-rules articles.

 


Can Execution Price Affect Daily Loss or Maximum Loss?
Yes. The final execution price affects the trade result and your account Equity.

 

If the account is close to the Daily Loss or Maximum Loss limit, even a small change in execution price or spread can cause Equity to cross the breach level.

Important: The balance shown after the trade is closed may not reflect the lowest Equity reached while the position was still open or being closed.

 

What Can Support Review?
Depending on the platform and information available, Support may review the account number, trade ticket, symbol, order direction, volume, trigger/requested price, final execution price and relevant timestamps.

 

The presence of slippage, spread movement or a different execution price does not by itself establish that a technical malfunction occurred.

Challenge and Funded accounts simulate real-market execution conditions.

Buy orders use Ask to open and Bid to close; Sell orders use Bid to open and Ask to close.

Slippage can occur in normal or volatile conditions and may be positive or negative.

Stop Loss is a trigger level and does not guarantee an exact execution price.

Spread, liquidity, order volume, gaps and latency can affect final execution.

Execution prices can affect Equity and drawdown monitoring.

Free Trial and Competition accounts do not apply simulated slippage or execution delay.

Instant Funding is available in four configurations, selected at purchase: your consistency choice (15% or 20%) combined with your profit split choice (60% or 88%) — i.e. 15% / 60%, 15% / 88%, 20% / 60%, or 20% / 88%.

 

What is the difference between 15% and 20% consistency on Instant Funding?

The 15% consistency option is cheaper, but requires tighter consistency across trading days. The 20% consistency option is easier to meet, but comes at a higher price.

 

Important: Instant Funding has no Minimum Profitable Days requirement.
The 15% and 20% consistency options can mathematically require profits to be spread across several days, but this is not the same as a Minimum Profitable Days rule. There is no requirement for an Instant Funding trading day to reach 0.5% of the initial account size.

 

How many days are needed mathematically?
20% consistency: your best day must not exceed 20% of the overall P&L for the current payout cycle. If profits are distributed evenly, this can be achieved with 5 profitable days or more.

15% consistency: your best day must not exceed 15% of the overall P&L. If profits are distributed evenly, this requires 7 profitable days or more.

 

Example - $100,000 Instant Funding account, 20% consistency
​
A trader makes $30 on each of five trading days:
Day 1: $30
Day 2: $30
Day 3: $30
Day 4: $30
Day 5: $30
​
Overall P&L = $150.
Best day = $30.
Consistency = $30 ÷ $150 = 20%.
​
Each $30 day is only 0.03% of a $100,000 account. The days do not need to reach 0.5%. The trader satisfies the 20% consistency calculation even though none of the five days would qualify as a 0.5% Minimum Profitable Day.

Example - $100,000 Instant Funding account, 15% consistency
​
A trader makes $30 on each of seven trading days.
Overall P&L = $210.
Best day = $30.
Consistency = $30 ÷ $210 = 14.29%.
​
This is below the 15% threshold. Again, each day is only 0.03% of the initial account size, and that is acceptable because Instant Funding has no 0.5% Minimum Profitable Days requirement.

 

What is the difference between 60% and 88% profit split on Instant Funding?

The 60% profit split option is available at a lower price, but you keep a smaller share of your profits. The 88% profit split option costs more, but you keep a larger share of your profits.

What is the difference between 15% and 20% consistency?

The 15% consistency option is offered at a lower price but requires tighter consistency across trading days. The 20% consistency option is easier to meet but comes at a higher price.

The 60% profit split option is offered at a lower price, but you keep a smaller share of your profits. The 88% profit split option costs more, but you keep a larger share of your profits.

Depending on the consistency option selected at purchase, no more than 15% or 20% of total profits may be generated within a single 24-hour trading period.

Account Violations
Daily Loss Limit
 Important Points:

The Daily Loss Limit is calculated based on your Balance or Equity, whichever is higher, at the rollover snapshot, minus the Daily Loss amount for your account size.

The Daily Loss amount is calculated based on your initial account size.

For Instant Funding, the Daily Loss amount is 3% of your initial account size. The rollover snapshot occurs every day at 10 PM UTC. At rollover, the system compares your Balance and Equity, uses the higher of the two, and subtracts your Daily Loss amount to set that day's Breach Level. A violation occurs if your Equity drops below the Breach Level at any point during the day.

 

Example ($100,000 account)
The Daily Loss amount = 3% of $100,000 (initial size) = $3,000.

On a fresh $100,000 account, the Breach Level is $100,000 − $3,000 = $97,000.

If your balance grows to $120,000, the Breach Level at the next rollover snapshot is $120,000 − $3,000 = $117,000. The amount stays $3,000 because it is based on your initial $100,000 size, not your current balance.

If your Equity falls below the Breach Level, the account is breached.

 

Important: For the full snapshot logic — including how the higher of Balance or Equity is used, and worked examples for trades held through rollover — please read How does my Daily Permitted Loss work. This article explains the Daily Loss Limit in full.

Maximum Loss Limit (Trailing)
The maximum loss limit is 5% of balance or equity, whichever is higher, across the lifetime of the account.

 

This limit is trailing and does not reset. If your equity drops below this level at any time, the account will be breached.

 

Note: The 5% maximum loss applies to Instant Funding accounts purchased on or after 2 July 2026. Instant Funding accounts purchased before 2 July 2026 retain a 6% maximum loss — subtract $6,000 instead of $5,000 in the steps below (starting maximum loss level of $94,000).

 

Example:
Let's say you have a $100,000 trading account with:

Trailing Drawdown: 5%

Starting Account Balance: $100,000

Step 0: 

Account Started Maximum loss allowed = $100,000 − $5,000 = $95,000

 

Step 1: Account Grows 

If you make a $5,000 profit, your account balance becomes $105,000. Maximum loss allowed = $105,000 − $5,000 = $100,000. Because this equals your starting balance, the trailing drawdown has reached its cap → $100,000

 

Step 2: Account Grows More 

If you then make another $10,000 profit, your account balance becomes $115,000. Calculated = $115,000 − $5,000 = $110,000, but the trailing drawdown cannot go above the original starting balance, so: Maximum loss allowed = $100,000

 

Step 3: Account Drops 

If your account falls to $100,000, the trailing drawdown will have been reached and the account will be breached.

Daily Loss Limit
Important Points:

The Daily Loss Limit is calculated based on your Balance or Equity, whichever is higher, at the rollover snapshot, minus the Daily Loss amount for your account size.

The Daily Loss amount is calculated based on your initial account size.

The Daily Loss amount is 5% (Standard configuration) or 4% (4% / 8% configuration) of your initial account size. The daily rollover snapshot occurs at 22:00 UTC. A violation occurs if your Equity drops below the calculated breach level at any point during the day.

 

Example (Standard 5% configuration, $100,000 account)
The Daily Loss amount = 5% of $100,000 (initial size) = $5,000.

On a fresh $100,000 account, the breach level is $100,000 − $5,000 = $95,000.

If your balance grows to $120,000, the breach level at the next rollover snapshot is $120,000 − $5,000 = $115,000 (the amount stays $5,000 because it is based on your initial $100,000 size, not your current balance).

If your equity falls below the breach level, the account is breached. (On the 4% configuration, the Daily Loss amount would be $4,000, so the breach level on a fresh account would be $96,000.)

Maximum Loss Limit (Static)
The maximum cumulative loss across all trading days is 10% (Standard configuration) or 8% (4% / 8% configuration) of your account, based on your initial account size.

 

This limit applies across the lifetime of the account (it is not a single-day limit). Exceeding it, including through floating losses, will result in an account breach. A violation occurs if your Equity drops below the calculated breach level.

 

Example ($100,000 account)
Standard (10%): your equity must not fall below $90,000 across all trading days.

4% / 8% configuration (8%): your equity must not fall below $92,000 across all trading days.

Max Floating Loss
On funded 2-Step accounts, a limit also applies to how much floating (unrealized) loss can build up on a single symbol or trade idea. This threshold depends on your drawdown configuration:

5% daily loss (Standard) configuration: 3% floating loss on the same symbol

4% daily loss configuration: 2% floating loss on the same symbol

Reaching this threshold closes the open trades on that symbol and moves the account to a stricter 1.5% risk-per-trade-idea limit; exceeding 1.5% after that results in a breach. For full details, see Max Floating Loss Allowed on Funded Accounts.

Think of it as a tiny step that shows how much the market moves.For most currency pairs, 1 pip = 0.0001.Example: If EURUSD moves from 1.10201 to 1.10211, that’s 1 pip movement.For pairs with the Japanese Yen, like USDJPY, 1 pip = 0.01. So, if USDJPY goes from 110.25 to 110.26, that’s also 1 pip.How to Calculate Pip?The value of a pip depends on:The currency pair you’re trading.The lot size (how many units you are trading).The currency of your account.Example:If you trade 1 standard lot (100,000 units) of EURUSD:A movement of 1 pip = 10 USD.If you trade 1 mini lot (10,000 units) of EURUSD:A movement of 1 pip = 1 USD.If you trade 1 micro lot (1,000 units) of EURUSD:A movement of 1 pip = 0.10 USDSince this calculation can be tricky, many traders use a pip calculator to quickly find out how much each pip is worth in their trade.Why Pip is Important?Pips are important because they help traders measure and compare market movements easily:Measuring Profits and Losses: If EURUSD moves 20 pips in your favor, you can calculate how much you gained.Risk Management: Traders often decide how many pips they are willing to risk before entering a trade.Universal Language: Whether you are in Asia, Europe, or America, traders everywhere understand pips. When someone says they earned 50 pips, it means the same thing globally.Other Glossary TermsPPipetteA pipette is one-tenth of a pip, used to measure smaller, more precise price movements in forex trading, giving traders a clearer view of market fluctuations on modern platforms.P&L (Profit and Loss)P&L (Profit and Loss) shows the result of a trade, indicating whether a trader gained or lost value based on the difference between entry and exit prices, adjusted for trade size.Price ActionPrice action is the study of a market’s price movement over time, where traders analyze charts, candlesticks, and key levels to make decisions without relying on technical indicators.Position SizePosition size is the amount of a financial instrument you buy or sell in one trade.

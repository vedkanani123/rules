You'll trade the same assets you're comfortable with: forex pairs, equity indices, commodities, or futures contracts, depending on the firm's platform. Strategy freedom exists, but within boundaries that reflect how institutional capital gets managed. Firms care whether your approach sustains profitability while respecting risk thresholds, not whether you favor five-minute charts over daily timeframes. That distinction matters because traders often confuse "any strategy" with "any behavior," and the gap between those two ideas determines whether you pass or get disqualified.

The flexibility lets you leverage existing strengths rather than forcing adaptation to unfamiliar markets. If you've spent years refining entries around London session volatility in EUR/USD, you can deploy that exact framework. Permitted Strategies

Short-term scalping thrives here when paired with disciplined risk controls. The rapid turnover helps some traders meet minimum trading-day requirements more quickly, building the three to five sessions needed for qualification while keeping individual trade risk minimal. Range-bound plays targeting oscillations between support and resistance zones qualify without issue. Restricted Strategies

High-frequency automated executions face outright bans across most evaluations. Latency arbitrage, where algorithms exploit microsecond delays between price feeds, is immediately flagged because it capitalizes on platform inefficiencies rather than market insight. Tick-by-tick scalping that opens and closes hundreds of positions per session similarly violates rules designed to assess human decision-making, not server proximity advantages. Firms monitor for these patterns through trade frequency analysis, and detection triggers instant disqualification regardless of profitability.

‍

Arbitrage plays that exploit price discrepancies across different instruments or venues fall outside acceptable bounds. These setups avoid genuine market exposure by locking in risk-free profits through simultaneous opposing positions, which undermines the evaluation's purpose of testing your ability to navigate volatility. Firms view this as gaming the system rather than demonstrating skill, making it grounds for termination, even if it is technically profitable.

‍

Mirroring trades from external signals or coordinating with other participants dilutes individual accountability, the core premise of these assessments. Firms track IP addresses, entry timing correlations, and position overlap to detect such behavior, enforcing solo decision-making to validate personal competence. Getting caught copying someone else's trades ends your trial immediately, with no refund on entry fees.

Hedging across multiple positions to neutralize directional risk gets prohibited in most one-step formats. Opening long and short positions simultaneously in correlated instruments might seem clever for managing exposure, but it masks whether you can make clear directional calls, the skill firms need to see. This restriction compels conviction in your setups rather than hedged bets that obscure true performance.

Progressive sizing schemes like martingale, where you double position size after losses to recoup quickly, qualify as reckless gambling. These approaches exponentially escalate drawdowns, directly conflicting with the 4% to 6% daily and 6% to 10% overall loss thresholds that govern evaluations. A single bad sequence can breach limits instantly, making this tactic a fast track to failure rather than recovery.

Tips for Passing a One-Step Evaluation

Passing demands more than hitting profit targets. You need execution precision that respects every boundary simultaneously: drawdown thresholds, daily loss caps, minimum trading days, and prohibited tactics. The difference between qualification and disqualification often comes down to whether you've internalized these constraints as reflexes rather than rules you check after the fact. Preparation separates those who pass on the first attempt from those who burn through multiple entry fees, learning the same lessons.

Strictly Adhere to Evaluation Guidelines

The ruleset governs everything, yet most traders skim documentation once and assume they've absorbed the critical details. Daily drawdown calculations vary between firms. Some measures are based on the starting balance; others are based on the peak equity reached during the session. That distinction determines whether a 4% pullback after a strong morning violates your cap or stays within bounds. Misunderstanding this single mechanic ends trials prematurely, regardless of your strategy's soundness.

‍

Create a reference sheet listing every parameter: profit target percentage, static versus trailing drawdown type, daily loss threshold, minimum active days, restricted trading windows around news releases, and prohibited position types like hedged pairs. Pin it where you'll see it before every session. When you're three trades deep and considering a fourth position, glancing at that sheet reminds you whether adding exposure keeps you compliant or pushes you over the edge. This isn't paranoia. Firms automate violation detection, and their systems don't grant grace periods for ignorance.

Implement Robust Risk Controls

Position sizing determines survival more than entry accuracy. Risking 1% per trade across a $100,000 evaluation account means each stop loss caps at $1,000. If your strategy targets 50-pip moves in EUR/USD with 20-pip stops, that translates to specific lot sizes you calculate before activation, not during live execution when emotions cloud math. Traders who skip this step routinely oversize positions, breaching daily caps after two consecutive losses instead of the five or six their plan should withstand.

‍

Stop placement needs mechanical consistency. Volatility-based methods like setting stops at 1.5 times the Average True Range adapt to current conditions, preventing tight stops during expanded ranges that trigger prematurely. Pairing this with a fixed risk-reward ratio, typically 1:2, helps ensure winners outpace losers even with modest win rates. A 40% success rate generates net gains when your average winner doubles your average loser, but only if you enforce these ratios without exception. Emotional exits that cut winners short or let losers run past predetermined stops destroy this balance faster than poor entries ever could.

Chasing the 8% target in the first week invites overtrading and oversized bets that breach risk limits. Segment the goal into manageable increments: 2% per week over four weeks, or roughly 0.4% per day across 20 trading sessions. This framing transforms an intimidating threshold into a series of modest benchmarks that compound without requiring heroic performance on any single day. Missing a daily target doesn't demand immediate recovery through doubled risk. The evaluation window provides enough time for consistent execution to accumulate results.

Trailing stops protect accumulated gains as trades develop. Entering a swing position with a 50-pip initial stop, then moving it to breakeven once price advances 50 pips, eliminates downside while preserving upside potential. This technique is especially valuable in the final stretch, when you're at 7% gains with a trailing drawdown that has tightened your risk buffer. A single unprotected reversal can erase days of progress, but locked-in profits ensure forward momentum even if subsequent setups fail.

What Is a Trading Simulator, and How Does It Work?
A trading simulator, sometimes called a demo trading account or paper trading account, lets you practise trading with virtual money instead of real funds. It connects to real or delayed market data, allowing you to place simulated trades under actual market conditions.

A handful of platforms provide a virtual balance ranging from $10,000 to $100,000, depending on the simulator.

The simulator tracks fills, calculates profit and loss, and reports performance exactly as a live account would, minus the financial consequence. Behind the scenes, a simulator's execution engine tries to replicate how a live broker would fill an order:

Match a market order against the current quote.
Check margin requirements.
Apply spreads or commissions if the platform models them.

The quality of this simulation varies enormously between platforms, which matters more than most beginners realize.

This gap explains something many beginners find frustrating: performing well in a simulator, then struggling once money enters the picture. While the mechanics stay identical, the psychology doesn't.
Why Beginners Should Start With a Simulator First
To practice trading with virtual money means building experience before risking your own capital. So, instead of worrying about losing money, you can focus on understanding price movements, testing strategies, and developing good trading habits.
For instance:

As a beginner, if you blow up a $500 live account in a week, there is a tendency to walk away from trading entirely. You feel convinced the whole thing was a scam or simply too hard.

If you blow up a simulated $100,000 account, you learn the same lesson for free and can reset immediately.

Simulators also build platform familiarity before it matters. Beginners learn where the order ticket sits, how to set a stop-loss, and how leverage multiplies both gains and losses. All of this is far better learned with fake money than during a live market.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)
How long should a beginner practice on a simulator before trading real money?
No universal number exists, though many experienced traders suggest at least 50-100 simulated trades logged with consistent rules before risking real capital. The real marker is whether a strategy shows repeatable results across different market conditions rather than one lucky stretch.
Do simulator results actually predict live trading success?
Partially. Simulators validate whether a strategy has a mathematical edge under clean conditions. They can't replicate the psychological pressure of real losses, so a trader performing well in simulation still needs a gradual transition to live trading.
Can a beginner use simulator results to qualify for a prop firm evaluation?
No, prop firm evaluations require live, real-money-equivalent performance on their own platform under their specific rules. Simulator practice builds the skill and discipline needed to pass an evaluation, but it doesn't substitute for the evaluation itself.
Is it better to use one simulator consistently or try several different ones?
Consistency with one platform generally builds stronger habits than platform-hopping, since switching tools resets familiarity with order types and interface layout.

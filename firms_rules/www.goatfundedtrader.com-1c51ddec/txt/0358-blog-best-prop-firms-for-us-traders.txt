Firms wishing to serve American traders must comply with a complex web of rules enforced by agencies like the Commodity Futures Trading Commission, or CFTC, and the National Futures Association, or NFA. These regulations demand rigorous registration, identity verification, anti-money laundering systems, and transparent risk disclosures. Many international prop firms find these requirements burdensome and costly, leading them to exclude US clients to avoid the risk of non-compliance.Legal Liability and InfrastructureLegal risks are higher when dealing with US clients. Foreign property firms may lack the necessary legal resources or infrastructure to handle disputes, regulatory investigations, or lawsuits involving U.S. residents. The US legal system is renowned for its complexity and the potential for significant penalties, which can deter some firms from accepting this exposure.Broker and Platform RestrictionsMany brokers and trading platform providers restrict US clients. Offshore brokers that prop firms partner with often have explicit terms prohibiting US residents due to their regulatory constraints. Additionally, recent actions by MetaQuotes, the provider of MetaTrader 4 and 5, have led to a crackdown on US-based prop trading. MetaQuotes has pressured firms to stop offering its platforms in the US to avoid regulatory issues, forcing many prop firms to suspend their US registrations or switch to alternative platforms, such as DXtrade, cTrader, and Match-Trader.Market Access LimitationsSome prop firms lack access to US-preferred markets. Certain asset classes, such as US futures contracts, are not readily available through all international brokers.

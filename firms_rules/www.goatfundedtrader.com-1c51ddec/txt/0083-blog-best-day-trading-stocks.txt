Traders engaging in this practice look to capitalize on small price changes that occur throughout the session rather than holding stocks for the long term. This short-term approach distinguishes day trading from traditional investing, where the focus is on the stock's value over months or years.Day traders aim to make profits by taking advantage of price volatility triggered by news, market events, or announcements. They closely analyse market patterns to find the best moments to enter and exit trades, often making multiple transactions in a single day. The goal is to accumulate smaller gains repeatedly rather than waiting for large profits over extended periods.One of the fundamental tactics in day trading is the use of margin and leverage. Instead of paying the full price for stocks, traders put down a fraction, known as margin, to control larger positions. While this can multiply profits, it also amplifies losses, making risk management crucial. Successful day traders often use stop-loss orders and limits to protect their capital from sudden, adverse price moves.Day trading requires quick decision-making and constant market monitoring.

Used deliberately, it scales a measurable edge; used carelessly, it turns small market moves into account‑ending events.How does that amplification feel in practice?Think of leverage as enlarging every decision you make. When prices move, your gains or losses are calculated based on the notional contract size, not just the collateral you posted, so intraday swings translate directly into margin impact and potential liquidations. Leverage in futures trading can be as high as 100:1, ITB Forex Broker, and that means tiny percentage moves can quickly change your required equity and force choices you did not plan for.Why do traders confuse leverage and margin so often?When we coached a group of sim traders through a 12-week program, the same mistake kept recurring: they treated margin as a buffer rather than as a pricing mechanism that changes every time the market marks to market. Margin is the collateral you post; leverage is the multiple of the collateral against the contract notional. That misunderstanding causes stress when exchanges raise margin requirements after volatility spikes, and it is why many traders suddenly face margin calls.What happens when volatility bites?Because futures are marked to market each session, realized and unrealized P&L alter your available margin in real time. A 1% change in the market can lead to a 100% gain or loss with 100:1 leverage, ITB Forex Broker. In plain terms, small moves can erase an account or double it, and maintenance margins tighten quickly under stress, often leaving no transparent window to deleverage manually.Most traders accept high leverage because it accelerates returns, and that approach works while markets behave as expected. Use stop distance, not leverage targets, to compute required margin and probable drawdown, because stops drive worst‑case loss, and margin rules determine whether that loss will force an early exit. This constraint‑first approach trades big thrills for repeatability.How does daily settlement change behavior?Mark‑to‑market turns yesterday’s unrealized profit into today’s usable margin and yesterday’s loss into an immediate shortfall. That mechanism rewards discipline: consistent modest wins compound, and a single day with an outsized loss can require fresh collateral or trigger forced liquidation. Treat daily settlement as a heartbeat you must monitor, not a background process. Start with the raw math, then translate it into position limits that respect your stop loss, margin cushion, and the correlation of other positions in the account.How do you get the raw numbers?Compute the contract notional, which is the contract size times the market price. Once you have contracts, compute effective leverage by dividing the trade notional by your account equity.

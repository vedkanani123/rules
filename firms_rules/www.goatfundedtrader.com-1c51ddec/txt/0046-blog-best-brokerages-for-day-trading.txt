According to this rule, traders who execute four or more day trades within a rolling five-business-day period are classified as pattern day traders. Such traders must maintain a minimum equity of $25,000 in their margin accounts at all times to continue day trading activities. If the account falls below this level, trading privileges are suspended until the balance is restored to the minimum requirement.This $25,000 threshold ensures that traders have enough capital to manage the high risks associated with frequent trades and to absorb potential losses. The rule is designed to protect both the trader and the broader financial market from the risks associated with excessive risk-taking without sufficient financial backing. Importantly, this rule applies only to margin accounts; cash accounts that settle trades with personal funds are exempt from this restriction.While some brokers might have their own variations of the rule, the federally mandated minimum balance remains $25,000 for pattern day traders. Traders with less frequent trading activity or those who do not meet the pattern day trading definition may not be subject to this minimum, allowing them to start with smaller accounts. However, it's vital to recognize that day trading itself is risky; studies show the vast majority of day traders lose money, partly because they compete against professionals and sophisticated algorithms.It's also worth noting that FINRA is discussing the potential lowering of this $25,000 requirement to as low as $2,000 to make day trading more accessible to small retail investors. However, this change has yet to be finalized and may take effect in late 2025 or early 2026, pending approval.For traders keen to start day trading today, having at least $25,000 in a margin account is essential if they intend to trade frequently,  meaning four or more trades within five business days.

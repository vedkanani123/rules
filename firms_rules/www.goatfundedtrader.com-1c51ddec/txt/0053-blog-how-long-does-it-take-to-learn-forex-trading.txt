You profit when the currency you buy strengthens against the one you sell. The market operates 24 hours a day across financial centers worldwide. According to Bank of America, the forex market handles $7.5 trillion in daily trading volume, making it the largest and most liquid financial market in the world.💡 Example: If you believe the Euro will strengthen against the US Dollar, you would buy the EUR/USD pair. When you buy EUR/USD at 1.1000, you're purchasing euros while selling dollars, betting the euro will strengthen. If the pair rises to 1.1100, you've captured 100 pips of profit—each pip represents a 0.0001 change in value. Major currency pairs like EUR/USD, USD/JPY, and GBP/USD dominate trading because their high liquidity creates tight spreads and predictable price movements, which respond sharply to economic data releases, interest rate decisions, and geopolitical events.Understanding Pips and Position SizingPips measure the smallest price change in forex, typically the fourth decimal place (0.0001) for most pairs or the second (0.01) for yen-based pairs. A move from 1.2050 to 1.2060 equals 10 pips. For a standard lot of 100,000 units, each pip translates to $10 in profit or loss. Position sizing determines how many lots you trade, directly controlling your exposure. A mini lot (10,000 units) reduces your pip value to $1, enabling tighter risk control.Leverage Changes Everything About RiskLeverage lets you control large positions with a small amount of money. With 50:1 leverage, a $2,000 deposit controls $100,000 in currency. This amplifies both gains and losses equally. A 1% favorable move generates significant profit, but a 1% adverse move can wipe out your margin without proper stop-loss discipline. Most beginners underestimate how quickly leverage can turn a manageable loss into a margin call.

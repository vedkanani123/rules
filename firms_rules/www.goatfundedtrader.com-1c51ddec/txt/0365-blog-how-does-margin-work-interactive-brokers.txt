You put up a percentage of the purchase price—usually 50%—while the broker lends the remainder, using your existing collateral. This leverage amplifies both potential gains and losses, magnifying market movements in your position.💡 Example: If you have $5,000 in cash and want to buy $10,000 worth of stock, you can use margin trading to borrow the additional $5,000 from your broker. Your $5,000 serves as collateral, and you now control a $10,000 position with half the capital.⚠️ Warning: While margin trading can amplify your profits, it equally amplifies your losses. A 10% drop in your investment becomes a 20% loss on your actual capital when using 2:1 leverage.How does borrowed capital multiply your purchasing power?With a margin account, you multiply your purchasing power. If you have $10,000 and want to buy stock at $100 per share, a cash account lets you control 100 shares. With margin, you put in $10,000 and borrow another $10,000, controlling 200 shares. If the stock rises to $110, your cash account gains $1,000 (10% return), while your margin position gains $2,000 on the same $10,000 of your own money (20% return before interest). A $10 drop wipes out 20% of your capital, not 10%.How does margin work at Interactive Brokers? According to Charles Schwab, the 50% initial margin requirement sets the standard for most equity purchases, though brokers can demand higher percentages for volatile or thinly traded securities.How Collateral and Equity Work TogetherYour securities act as collateral securing the loan, and your account equity—the market value of holdings minus the borrowed balance—determines your financial cushion. As stock prices fluctuate, this equity changes constantly. A rising market increases your equity; a falling market shrinks it. Brokers watch this ratio closely because their money is at risk. If your equity drops too low, the firm issues a margin call demanding immediate deposits to restore the required balance. Fail to meet it, and the broker automatically sells your positions to recover the loan, locking in your losses at the worst possible moment.What makes margin trading so appealing to traders?Traders use margin to amplify moderate beliefs into larger returns without having to deploy all their capital.

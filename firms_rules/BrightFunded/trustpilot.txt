Dear Trader,

Thank you for sharing your experience. We want to clarify how our account types work, since trailing drawdown is one of the most misunderstood aspects of a prop firm account, and getting the facts right may change how this looks to you.

We offer three account types, each with a different drawdown structure. The One-Step account uses a trailing maximum drawdown of three percent on the evaluation phase and six percent once funded, meaning the drawdown limit moves upward with your account equity as it reaches new highs. The Two-Step Bright account uses a static maximum drawdown of four percent on the evaluation phase and eight percent once funded, meaning the limit is fixed to your starting balance and does not move as your equity grows. The Two-Step Classic account uses a static maximum drawdown of five percent on the evaluation phase and ten percent once funded, offering the most room of the three.

Traders tend to prefer the One-Step account because it is a single evaluation phase rather than two, which means a faster route to a funded account and typically a lower cost of entry. The trade-off for that speed is the trailing structure, which is stricter because the drawdown ceiling rises with the account rather than staying fixed. This is not a rule that changes after the fact. It is documented before purchase, and it applies equally to every trader on that plan.

If a trailing drawdown does not suit your trading style, particularly if you tend to let winning trades run before pulling profit, the Two-Step Bright or Two-Step Classic accounts may be a better fit, since the static drawdown gives you a fixed buffer that does not tighten as your balance increases. Both remain fully available to you, and switching to a structure that matches how you trade is a legitimate choice rather than a compromise.

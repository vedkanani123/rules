That frustrating difference between the price you wanted and the price you got is slippage, and it can happen even when you do everything right. Let’s break down what slippage is in trading and the practical ways you can reduce it in real market conditions.
TL;DR

Slippage is the difference between the expected trade price and the actual executed price.
Slippage can be positive or negative, sometimes improving and other times worsening your execution price.
It’s most prevalent during high market volatility or low liquidity.
You can’t fully eliminate slippage in live markets, but smart order types, trading timing, and risk methods help reduce it.

What Is Slippage in Trading?
Slippage refers to the difference between the price at which you intend to enter or exit a trade and the price at which your order is actually filled.
For example, if a buy order is placed at 1.10500 but executed at 1.10520 due to price changes between submission and fulfillment, the 2-pip difference is slippage.
Why Slippage Occurs
Slippage is a natural part of live markets. It happens when liquidity is insufficient at your requested price or when price shifts happen quickly before an order executes.
Common causes include:

Market volatility during news or events.
Low liquidity, such as outside peak hours or in thin markets.
Large order sizes that eat through available prices.
Execution delays from platforms or connectivity.

Types of Slippage

Positive slippage: means the trade filled at a better-than-expected price.
Negative slippage: means the execution price was worse than expected, hurting performance.

Both types can occur during slippage in trading, and while negative slippage is more common, positive slippage can occasionally improve your trade outcome.
How Slippage Works in CFDs Trading
CFDs (Contracts for Difference) are derivatives tied to an underlying asset’s price movements. Like other financial instruments, CFDs trades are subject to market conditions, meaning slippage can occur when prices are moving quickly or there’s low liquidity.
While trading CFDs, you need to consider slippage, especially when trading volatile instruments like forex pairs or commodities.
The Impact of Slippage on Your Trading Performance
Slippage affects profitability and risk management:

Increased Costs: Negative slippage increases the cost of entering or exiting positions.
Strategy Performance: Frequent slippage can erode expectations, especially for scalpers and high-frequency traders.
Unintended Exits: Stop losses and take profits might execute at different levels than you planned.

While slippage cannot be eliminated, knowing how to manage it can improve trade outcomes.
How to Avoid Slippage in Trading
The right order choices and timing can help you control execution and limit unexpected price shifts.
1. Use Limit Orders Instead of Market Orders

Limit orders let you set the exact price you want; the order only executes at that price or better.
Market orders execute at the best available price, which can trigger slippage.

2. Trade During High Liquidity Periods

High liquidity means more buyers and sellers at tight prices.
For forex CFDs, peak overlaps, such as London–New York, typically have higher liquidity.

3. Avoid Major News Events

Volatility spikes around news releases (e.g., interest rate decisions, employment reports) can increase slippage risk.

4. Monitor Market Conditions

Track economic calendars and price action before sending orders.
Avoid sending large orders that could clear available prices.

Slippage in Simulated vs. Live Markets
At FundedNext, slippage exists in both demo and funded accounts because it reflects real market conditions and price movement dynamics. We replicate live conditions to give traders realistic execution experiences.
Slippage is usually a normal execution effect, but poor execution quality can also reflect broker or platform issues.
Final Thoughts
Understanding what slippage is in trading, why it happens, and how it affects CFD execution is essential for every trader. While you cannot eliminate slippage completely, you can reduce its impact with smarter order types, better timing, and disciplined risk management. The goal is not perfect execution every time, but consistent decision-making in real market conditions, so your strategy stays in control even when the market moves fast.
Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)
What is slippage in CFD?
Slippage in CFDs is when a CFD order fills at a different price than expected due to market movement and liquidity gaps.
Is slippage good or bad for traders?
Slippage can be both: positive slippage gives better pricing, while negative slippage may worsen your executed price.
Is 1% slippage good?
Whether 1% slippage is “good” depends on strategy and risk tolerance. Generally, lower slippage is preferable for consistent performance.
How can traders reduce slippage?
Use limit orders, trade during high liquidity, avoid major news, and select brokers with fast execution.
Is slippage the same as spread?
No. Spread is the difference between bid and ask prices before trading, while slippage is a post-execution price difference.
Is slippage allowed in prop firm trading?
Yes. Slippage is a common execution effect, especially during volatility, and can occur in both demo and live conditions.
How to avoid slippage in trading?
You can’t completely avoid it, but you can reduce it with disciplined order types, timing, and market awareness.

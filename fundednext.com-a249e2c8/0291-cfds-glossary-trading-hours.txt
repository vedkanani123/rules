They define when prices update, orders can fill, and when daily events (like swap/rollover) occur.Why Trading Hours MatterLiquidity & spreads: Active hours usually mean tighter spreads and easier fills; quiet hours can mean wider spreads.Volatility: Big moves often happen when major trading sessions overlap.Costs & rollover: Overnight forex trading hours include a daily swap/rollover; know when your platform applies it.Where to Check Trading Hours Today (On Your Platform)Symbol/contract specifications: Open the symbol info to see trading hours today for that instrument.Server time awareness: Hours are shown in the platform’s server time, convert if needed.Maintenance breaks: Many symbols have a short daily pause when trading is closed.Typical Hours by InstrumentCurrency CFDs (often called “forex hours” / “forex market hours”): Trade nearly 24 hours, Monday–Friday with a brief daily maintenance break and a daily rollover. Your exact forex trading hours/forex trading time are in the symbol specs.Index CFDs (example: NASDAQ): Nasdaq trading hours (regular session) are tied to the exchange’s local schedule (e.g., the New York regular session). Many brokers also offer out‑of‑session pricing based on related futures. Always verify the exact CFD window in your platform.Metals (example: XAUUSD): XAUUSD trading hours are typically nearly 24 hours on weekdays with a short daily break. Check the symbol page for the precise open/close.Futures‑linked CFDs: Futures trading hours vary by contract. Many trade almost 23 hours per weekday with a short maintenance break. Your CFD follows the reference contract’s schedule as mapped by the broker.Common Global Session TimesThese trading session times help you plan when markets are busier:Asia (Tokyo): roughly 00:00–09:00 GMTEurope (London): roughly 07:00–16:00 GMTU.S. (New York): roughly 12:00–21:00 GMT Overlaps (e.g., London-New York.) often bring more activity; useful when thinking about trading hours and index CFDs.Trading Hours ExamplesCurrency example (EURUSD): You place a trade at 1.10201 during an active overlap. Liquidity is higher, so spreads are often tighter than late‑day quiet periods.Gold example (XAUUSD): You plan to hold after the daily break. Knowing XAUUSD trading hours helps you avoid placing orders during the maintenance window.Quick Tips for BeginnersAlways read the symbol’s trading hours in your platform before trading.Note maintenance breaks and the daily rollover time.Trade during busier sessions if you want generally tighter spreads.Convert to server time so your journal and fills line up with platform timestamps.Other Glossary TermsTTake ProfitA take profit (TP) is an automated order that closes a trade once the market hits your target price, helping you secure gains without constantly monitoring the charts.Technical AnalysisTechnical analysis is a rules-based study of price charts, patterns, and indicators used to plan precise entries, exits, and risk levels by analyzing past price behavior to guide future trades.Trade ExecutionTrade execution is the process of converting your buy or sell order into an actual filled trade at the best available price, covering everything from order placement to fill confirmation.TickA tick is the smallest possible price movement on a trading platform, representing the minimum step a price can move, helping traders set precise entries, stops, and targets.Trailing StopA trailing stop is a dynamic stop order that automatically adjusts as price moves in your favor, locking in gains and protecting profits if the market reverses.Show MoreStart yourFundedNext challengeThousands of traders are already getting rewarded by FundedNext.

It’s the time used on tickets, statements, and reports to show when a trade actually happened.Where You’ll See Trade TimeOrder tickets & fills: shows when your buy/sell was executed.Account history & statements: lists trade times for opens and closes.Exports & logs: sometimes labeled “Trade time” or “Fill time.”(Platforms record trade time in the platform’s server time. Convert if you journal in GMT or local time.)Why Trade Time MattersProof of execution: Confirms the moment your order filled (useful for slippage checks).Journaling & review: Helps you time trade entries/exits and see which time works best for you to trade.Costs & rollover: Determines which day’s swap/financing applies if you hold past the platform’s cutoff.Rules & limits: Some programs check breaches or targets based on trade time and server time.How Trade Time Differs from Other TimesOrder time vs trade time: You might click at 13:41:59, but the trade executes (trade time) at 13:42:05.Trading hours meaning: The window when a symbol can trade. Trade time is a moment inside that window.Session times: Asia/Europe/U.S. session clocks explain typical activity; trade time is your exact fill within those sessions.Trade Time ExampleYou place a market buy on EURUSD at 1.10201.Order time: 13:41:59Fill (trade time): 13:42:05 at 1.10203 (2 ticks higher)Your journal records: Trade time = 13:42:05, Entry = 1.10203.Later you close at 1.10401 with trade time = 15:18:20. These precise times help you analyze time trading patterns (e.g., which minutes around a session you perform best).Finding Trading Hours for Each SymbolTo act at the right trade time, first know the trading hours meaning for your instrument:Currencies (often called “forex hours” in guides): usually nearly 24 hours on weekdays with a daily break/rollover.Indices (e.g., Nasdaq) & Metals (e.g., XAUUSD): have specific open/close windows mapped by the broker. Check the symbol’s contract/specifications page in your platform for trading sessions and maintenance breaks.Quick Tips About Trade TimeJournal the exact trade time (with timezone) for every open and close.Align with your edge: If your wins cluster at certain trade times (e.g., right after a session opens), focus there.Convert timezones once: Pick GMT or your local time and stick to it in your journal.Plan around sessions: Use known trading session times to decide your time to trade each day.Other Glossary TermsTTake ProfitA take profit (TP) is an automated order that closes a trade once the market hits your target price, helping you secure gains without constantly monitoring the charts.Technical AnalysisTechnical analysis is a rules-based study of price charts, patterns, and indicators used to plan precise entries, exits, and risk levels by analyzing past price behavior to guide future trades.Trade ExecutionTrade execution is the process of converting your buy or sell order into an actual filled trade at the best available price, covering everything from order placement to fill confirmation.TickA tick is the smallest possible price movement on a trading platform, representing the minimum step a price can move, helping traders set precise entries, stops, and targets.Trailing StopA trailing stop is a dynamic stop order that automatically adjusts as price moves in your favor, locking in gains and protecting profits if the market reverses.Show MoreStart yourFundedNext challengeThousands of traders are already getting rewarded by FundedNext.

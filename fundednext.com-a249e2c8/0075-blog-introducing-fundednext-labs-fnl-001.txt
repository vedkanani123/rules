How the 40% Consistency Rule Works in the Challenge
The consistency rule ensures that no single trading day accounts for more than 40% of your total profit target. For the $50K account, the profit target is $3,000, so the single-day threshold is $1,200 ($3,000 × 40%).
Here is how it plays out in practice:

A day’s profit is $1,200 or less: Your target stays at $3,000.
A day’s profit exceeds $1,200, for example $1,400: the profit target adjusts so that day fits within 40% of the total. The new required profit becomes $1,400 ÷ 40% = $3,500.
A later day exceeds the previous best, for example $1,450: the target adjusts again to $1,450 ÷ 40% = $3,625.

There is no breach state here. The consistency rule cannot fail your account, close it, or void your attempt. The only consequence is a higher profit target. At $3,625, you need $625 more than the original $3,000 before you pass, traded on the same account under the same $2,000 MLL. The target stops moving as soon as your best day stops growing. The rule exists so a passing result reflects repeatable performance rather than one outsized session. It applies during the challenge only. The FundedNext Account has no consistency rule.
How the EOD Trailing Maximum Loss Limit Works
The Maximum Loss Limit is the total amount your account can lose before it is restricted from further trading. On FNL:001 the MLL is $2,000 and it trails on an end-of-day basis. It recalculates once per day from your closing balance, not tick by tick during the session.
Your starting floor: $48,000
A $50,000 account with a $2,000 MLL opens with a minimum allowed balance of $48,000. That is the only number in play until you close a day in profit.
How the floor moves, day by day
Day 1, a profitable day. You close $1,000 up at $51,000.
Calculation: $51,000 − $2,000 = $49,000
Result: your floor moves up to $49,000.
Day 2, the floor reaches its lock point. Another $1,100 takes the balance to $52,100.
Calculation: $52,100 − $2,000 = $50,100
Result: $50,100 is your starting balance plus $100, so the MLL locks here permanently. It will not trail higher regardless of how far the account grows.
Day 3, a losing day. The balance falls $600 to $51,500.
Result: the floor does not follow. It stays at $50,100.
Day 4, a strong day. The balance climbs $3,500 to $55,000.
Result: the floor is still $50,100. Your drawdown room is now $4,900, more than double what you started with, because the floor stopped trailing while the balance kept rising.
The number to remember is $52,100. Once your balance closes there or higher, trailing stops for the life of the account and your floor sits at $50,100, just above where you started. Every dollar earned after that widens your buffer instead of moving the limit.

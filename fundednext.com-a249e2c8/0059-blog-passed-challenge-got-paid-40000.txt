Thirty Years Later.A Mentee Watching the Same ThingWhat the Numbers ShowWatch The Full StoryMost traders carry a reason behind the screens. For Michael “Tom” Teodoro, the reason was a pickup truck.
Tom grew up as the only son in a family of four children in Pampanga, Philippines. When his father died in 2013, Tom was 17. His mother took over their landscaping and gardening business. For three decades, she ran it without a vehicle. You survive, Tom says. But it is tough. Somewhere between that reality and a college classroom, he made himself a promise.
The Person Behind the Setup
Tom studied information technology at Systems Plus College Foundation, majoring in networking. He built a career in IT, worked in Canada, returned to the Philippines for two years, then went back. That second stint in Canada is where trading became serious.
In 2025, he was spending 14 to 16 hours a day studying charts and market structure. His approach was academic. He had studied quantitative finance to understand price behaviour beyond patterns alone. A former business partner, Frank, who introduced him to crypto before passing away in 2022, had taught him one thing Tom carried forward: stay open to opportunity, because few people can actually see it when it is there.
Before choosing FundedNext, Tom researched. He had heard from Filipino traders that payouts were processed within hours of a request. FundedNext has paid over $306 million to traders and holds a zero payout denial rate. For a trader who had seen enough of this industry to be cautious, that was not a marketing claim. It was a verifiable number.
The FundedNext Challenge Account, and What Came After
Tom passed the FundedNext Challenge account. He moved to his FundedNext Account. Then he ran into something the evaluation phase had not prepared him for.
He was not processing his rewards.
He calls it a psychological wall. The pressure of a FundedNext Account changes how you see the market. His response was deliberate: six to seven months away from trading. Not a collapse of discipline. A planned reset. He needed to see the market without that pressure attached to every decision.
His wife Pamela held the household steady through that period. She went to work. She came home. She did not interrupt him. No pressure, no intervention. She gave him the space to recalibrate.
When Tom came back, the results followed.
$40,000. A Truck. Thirty Years Later.
FundedNext paid Tom $40,000 in 2025.
He bought his mother a pickup truck.
After 30 years of running a landscaping business without a vehicle, the business had one. Pamela was direct about what that moment meant to her: watching him make that purchase, she was proud of him. The income from trading gave their family certainty it had not had before.
The consistency of rewards through 2025 mattered as much as the individual amounts. It was proof that his approach held, and that the system he was operating inside was reliable. Rules visible from the start. Payouts that processed when he requested them.
A Mentee Watching the Same Thing
Tom now passes on what he built. One of his students, King Joshua Santiago, is 23 years old and a college student in Guagua, Pampanga. His read on the mentorship is worth quoting plainly: it gave him discipline, focus, and a clearer picture of trading as a profession.
King Joshua had spent three years watching people in trading communities rely on copy trading. He saw no consistent progress from any of them. His conclusion was direct: trading is a profession. It requires real work, step by step. It is not a shortcut.
That framing reflects what FundedNext is. The FundedNext Challenge account sets rules that are visible before you buy, a FundedNext Account that is yours to trade once you have earned it, and payouts that process at a stated rate. Traders get access to capital and a system they can understand completely.
What the Numbers Show
FundedNext has paid over $306 million to traders. The payout denial rate is zero. Most reward requests process within hours.
These are not claims that require trust. They are numbers that can be checked.
Tom’s $40,000 reward is one entry in that total. The truck is what that entry bought. A business that had operated for 30 years finally had a vehicle.
That is the documented outcome. One trader, one account, one result that was specific to his work and his approach. Not a promise of the same for anyone else.

Their primary goal is to profit from short-term price movements, avoiding holding positions overnight to minimize risk. This requires sharp focus and rapid decision-making. Before the market opens, day traders study pre-market data, news events, and economic indicators that shape market sentiment. This preparation helps them understand which assets might experience volatility and liquidity, crucial for quick trades during market hours. Day traders rely heavily on technical analysis. They use tools like moving averages, volume analysis, and momentum oscillators to decide when to enter or exit trades. Chart patterns and real-time data guide their actions, helping identify price trends and reversals.There are various day trading strategies. Momentum traders follow strong market trends to ride price movements, while scalpers make many small trades to capture tiny price changes. Day traders set strict stop-loss orders and profit targets to protect their capital. Discipline in sticking to these limits helps them avoid significant losses in the fast-moving market environment. Retail traders depend mainly on trading profits and face wide month-to-month swings in income.

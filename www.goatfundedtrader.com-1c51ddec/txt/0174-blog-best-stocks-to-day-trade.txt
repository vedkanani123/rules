Look for rising volume, widening spreads, and leading indicators, such as moving average crossovers, an RSI above 60, or a strong MACD. Entry techniques include buys on pullbacks to the moving average or entries after a breakout with confirmation from time and sales. Manage the trade with trailing stop loss levels and scale out as you lock in gains.Scalping: Tiny Gains, Big DisciplineScalping aims for small profits repeated many times during the session. Scalpers need low commissions, fast order execution, and stocks with consistent tick movement and tight spreads. Focus on Level II quotes, use small position sizes, and keep a strict stop loss for every trade. Which stocks work best for scalping? Typically, high liquidity, narrow spreads, and predictable intraday patterns like blue chip momentum names or stable ETFs.Breakout Trading: Enter When Price Clears Key LevelsBreakout traders wait for the price to clear an established resistance or support level, accompanied by confirmatory volume. Validate the breakout by observing increased average daily volume per minute, expanding order flow, and a reduced bid-ask spread. Beware of false breakouts; use retest entries when the price pulls back to the breakout level. Size small initial entries and add on confirmation. Use stop loss placement under the breakout level to control downside risk.Swing Concepts Applied Intraday: Hold for Hours, Not MinutesSome day traders use swing ideas inside a single session by holding positions for several hours to catch a complete intraday move. Select stocks with steady intraday trends and sufficient volatility so that a few hours give a meaningful price change. Monitor market internals and sector strength to avoid sudden reversals, and plan exits at technical inflection points, such as moving averages or prior swing highs.Arbitrage: Find Price Differences Across VenuesArbitrage captures temporary price gaps between exchanges, ETFs, and underlying basket mispricings, as well as related securities such as ADRs and foreign listings. Execution speed and access to multiple liquidity venues matter. Retail traders can identify small inefficiencies between an ETF and its underlying basket or between options and stocks. Still, they must also account for commissions, borrowing costs, and the risk of execution failure. Maintain a watchlist and a trading plan with clearly defined entry and exit rules. Log every trade, including reasons and outcomes, and periodically review edge and expectancy. Day traders rely on volatility because it creates the price action needed to generate profits within hours or minutes. Stocks with bigger and more frequent intraday price swings offer more opportunities to enter and exit trades at favourable prices. At the same time, low-volatility stocks tend to move too slowly to capture meaningful short-term gains.Focus on Relative VolumeRelative volume compares a stock’s current trading volume to its average volume over a set period, commonly the past several days or weeks. When relative volume spikes above typical levels, often at least twice the normal, it signals unusual activity and increased market interest. At these moments, stocks tend to be more volatile and offer better opportunities for day traders to capitalize on rapid price changes, supported by higher liquidity.Monitor Market-Moving NewsMarket news, such as earnings reports, mergers, product launches, or regulatory developments, often creates significant price movements by altering investor sentiment and expectations. Trading these stocks around news catalysts increases the likelihood of capturing quick gains as the market reacts to the news.Evaluate the Stock’s FloatA stock’s float indicates how many shares are available for public trading, excluding those held by insiders or locked-in investors. Stocks with a low float naturally have fewer shares available, which can lead to rapid and significant price swings, as buying or selling pressure affects supply and demand more dramatically. Many day traders seek low-float stocks because this scarcity can amplify price moves, particularly when combined with news or high volume.Use Technical Analysis ToolsTechnical indicators provide vital insights into price trends, momentum, and potential reversal points. Tools such as moving averages smooth out price data, while the Relative Strength Index (RSI) highlights overbought or oversold conditions. Bollinger Bands measure volatility and price boundaries. Positive sentiment drives demand and price increases, while negative sentiment can trigger sell-offs. Day traders track sentiment indicators, such as the VIX or specialized surveys, to gauge the market mood and adapt their trading strategies accordingly. Aligning trades with prevailing sentiment helps traders avoid fighting against dominant trends, thereby enhancing their trade success rates.Define Clear Trade Entry and Exit StrategiesSetting precise entry and exit points before placing a trade is critical to prevent emotional decision-making.

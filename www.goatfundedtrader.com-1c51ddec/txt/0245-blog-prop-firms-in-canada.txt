Traders must first pass an evaluation process designed to test their trading skills and risk management abilities. Once they complete this evaluation, they become funded traders who can trade using the firm's money, sharing a portion of the profits generated with the firm. The business model of Canadian prop firms typically involves several key steps. First, traders apply and pay an evaluation fee to participate in a challenge or test phase. This phase often uses simulated accounts that mirror real market conditions. The evaluation assesses the trader’s ability to follow risk rules and generate consistent profits. If the trader passes, they gain access to a live funded account with the firm’s capital. Profits earned are then split between the trader and the prop firm according to a pre-agreed percentage. Regarding the application process, Canadian traders usually select a prop firm, submit an application, and pay an upfront fee for the evaluation. These fees cover the cost of the evaluation service and risk management by the firm. Payment methods accepted include debit and credit cards, bank transfers, cryptocurrencies, and e-wallets. The evaluation itself may have specific rules, such as maximum drawdowns and profit targets, that traders must meet to qualify for funding. Payout structures vary, but generally involve profit sharing, where the trader receives a significant portion of the profits (often between 50% and 80%). At the same time, the prop firm retains the rest as compensation for providing capital and infrastructure. Withdrawals can be subject to conditions such as minimum payout thresholds or frequency limits, depending on the firm's policies. Some firms offer different account types or plans, with more advanced plans allowing quicker withdrawals or higher risk limits. Legally, prop firms operate in Canada by maintaining a clear distinction from traditional investment management. They do not handle client funds for investment purposes but instead offer evaluation services and trade solely with their proprietary capital. This structure allows them to comply with Canadian financial regulations without needing registration as securities dealers.
